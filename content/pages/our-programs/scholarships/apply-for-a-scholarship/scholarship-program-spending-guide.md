    title: What your scholarship can and can't be spent on
    yes:
      title: How you can use your scholarship
      items:
        - School or course fees
        - Laptops and computer equipment
        - Stationery
        - Textbooks
        - Public transport
        - Uniforms and clothing
        - School camps and excursions
        - Study furniture
        - Tutoring
        - Special equipment
    no:
      title: What your scholarship does not cover
      items:
        - "**Previous expenses and debt.** You should not use your scholarship to cover backdated educational expenses and debt from 2022 and earlier."
        - "**Expenses for family members.** The scholarship cannot be used to pay for education or training expenses for other family members or anyone other than the scholarship recipient."
        - "**HECS-HELP, FEE-HELP, university fees and non-government school fees.** These items are not covered."
        - "**Expenses not related to education.** For example: mobile phone, mobile phone plan, smart watches, electronics not directly related to education, internet bill over $300 total for your household, expenditure related to a personal vehicle, fuel, bicycle, clothing items, including shoes valued over $150 per item."
    note: "**If you are unsure about an item, please contact us.**"

  - type: features
    id: how-you-can-use-it
    nav: What's covered
    tone: card
    title: How you can use your scholarship
    items:
      - icon: book
        title: School or course fees
        text: Your scholarship covers school and course fees, except where HECS-HELP or FEE-HELP are available. University fees cannot be paid for with the scholarship and non-government school fees are also not covered. If you attend a special development school, the scholarship can go toward your fees.
      - icon: tools
        title: Laptops and computer equipment
        text: |
          Your scholarship can be used for a laptop or desktop computer. If your school requires you to use a device, such as an iPad or tablet, you can also use the scholarship for this.

          If you need a laptop for basic word processing, you should be able to find one for as little as $500. Printers, scanners and other computer-related equipment for your course are covered. You can also put your scholarship toward printing costs at school, TAFE or Uni.
      - icon: document
        title: Stationery
        text: Paper, exercise books, pens, pencils, erasers, rulers, and other stationery are covered by your scholarship.
      - icon: book
        title: Textbooks
        text: Textbooks directly related to your study or job training are covered.
      - icon: map
        title: Public transport
        text: |
          Public transport costs for travelling to and from school, your course or job training are covered by your scholarship.

          If you don't have public transport in your area, you can use the scholarship for other transport costs such as the school bus or parking.
      - icon: shield
        title: Uniforms and clothing
        text: You can use your scholarship to buy school uniforms and other clothing related to your course or job training.
      - icon: pin
        title: School camps and excursions
        text: Your scholarship can be used to pay for excursions, day trips and school camps related to your course or training. Non-compulsory excursions can be covered when there is a significant benefit to your education or training goals.
      - icon: home
        title: Study furniture
        text: Furniture related directly to the young person's ability to learn at home, e.g., desk and chair.
      - icon: people
        title: Tutoring
        text: Professional tutoring directly linked to the young person's education.
      - icon: key
        title: Special equipment
        text: If you have special requirements, your scholarship can be used to buy equipment to assist you in your studies or training. But before you use your scholarship, check whether you are eligible for support for special equipment from other organisations and services. For example, the Department of Families, Fairness and Housing (DFFH) offers programs to support people with disabilities.
