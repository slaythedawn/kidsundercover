ESSAY HINTS TO COMPLETE A SCHOLARSHIP
APPLICATION THROUGH KIDS UNDER COVER

1. READ THE DIRECTIONS
Take a little time to go over the requirements. Check to see if you’re eligible for the scholarship and
what you need to do to submit your application. Check the deadline, submission page and any other
important information you need. Pay particular attention to the purpose of the scholarship and use this
purpose to guide your written response.

2. USE A GREAT HOOK
Your introduction is how you grab onto the reader right out of the gate. When writing your responses,
have a strong introduction. This can help you stand out among a crowded field of applicants.
Think about things you’ve read that hooked you right away, like books or articles that have stayed
with you a long time. Starting your essay with a surprising fact, compelling anecdote or unexpected
question can grab the reader’s attention and make a good impression right away.
If you’re unsure about what a good hook looks like, take time to read sample scholarship responses
online and note what you like about them. Even bad ones can help you see what you should avoid
when writing.

3. GET PERSONAL
When appropriate, don’t be afraid to share personal stories. Your unique stories are what can set you
apart from the crowd.
Whether it’s about your education, your lived experiences, your goals or how the scholarship will help
you, giving specific examples of how the award relates to your life is more impactful than simply
explaining why you deserve to win.

4. BE POSITIVE
Detailing hardships may be difficult for you to share, but showcasing how you overcame those
hardships (or plan to with the help of this scholarship) and your positivity regarding your future is
important.
Try to leave the reader feeling hopeful about your future by staying positive throughout your essay,
even if you’re talking about hard topics or feelings.

5. GET SOMEONE TO TAKE A SECOND LOOK
After you’ve written and revised your essay, it’s still not done. Ask a friend, relative or teacher/mentor
to give your responses a second look. They can come at it with a fresh pair of eyes and let you know
what’s missing (if anything at all). Everyone needs an editor, so use your network to see what
improvements could be made.
