MESSAGE FROM THE CEO/CHAIR
We are excited to present to you our FY25 streamlined format for annual impact reporting.

FY25 was a year of both challenge and progress. Against a backdrop of worsening housing affordability, increasing concern about youth crime, and a
stretched out-of-home care system, the importance and urgency of Kids Under Cover’s work has never been clearer.

Despite these pressures, we continued to deliver strong impact, innovate in early intervention housing, and advocate for systemic change. We
strengthened partnerships, expanded our housing programs, and elevated our national voice.

A major milestone this year was securing multi-year Victorian Government funding for our Studio Program for the first time. During the year, we
delivered 55 studios, 35 in Victoria and 20 in South Australia, with only two builds remaining to complete our South Australian commitment under our
SAHA funding agreement. Our Village21 model also continues to grow, with two First Nations-led projects progressing well in Bairnsdale (to launch
January 2026) and Shepparton (to launch June 2026).

Innovation remains central to our approach. The Mobile Pod Program Pilot has expanded statewide, with pods being rolled out and additional units
ordered to meet urgent housing needs on sites constrained due to access limitations. An independent evaluation is underway to measure impact and
guide future scaling of the program and to form a basis for advocacy and business case to Government for funding to address critical housing needs
for young people at imminent risk of homelessness.

Education remains a cornerstone of our homelessness prevention approach. In November, 305 Department of Families, Fairness and Housing
scholarships, valued at $335,500 were awarded across Victoria, following strong demand from schools and TAFEs, while 119 Kids Under Cover
scholarships were awarded in the FY25.

Our annual Carers Survey reaffirmed the transformative impact of our work. Eighty-two per cent of studio recipients reported high satisfaction,
describing the accommodation as life-changing. Carers consistently highlighted reduced overcrowding, with 35 per cent reporting household conflict
was completely eliminated. Studios also supported independence, wellbeing and education, strengthening family relationships and helping young
people remain safely at home.

Kids Under Cover has continued to strengthen its role as a thought leader, contributing to key advisory groups and establishing a Leaving Care
Housing Incubator in partnership with Bridgeit to develop supported housing solutions for young people leaving care. We are also progressing plans to
embed lived experience through a Youth Advisory Group and Young Ambassadors program.

We thank our Board for their guidance, the senior leadership team and all staff for their dedication and impact. We are deeply grateful to our
government partners, donors, philanthropists and supporters, your commitment enables young people and families to access the stability and hope
they deserve.

YOUTH HOMELESSNESS IN 2025
Youth homelessness in Australia remains a growing and urgent challenge. The latest AIHW Specialist Homelessness
Services (SHS) 2024–25 data shows that increasing numbers of young people are entering homelessness alone, in
crisis, and without the support systems they need to stay safe and connected.

This is the reality:

40,500 children and young people sought homelessness support alone in 2024–25.
That is 1 in 7 people accessing homelessness services in Australia.

In Victoria, 11,359 young people experienced homelessness, the second-highest number in the country.

72% were not enrolled in education or training.

Homelessness at this age fractures safety, learning, and connection, often at the moment they are needed most.

These are the pathways into homelessness for young people.
    Family and domestic violence.
    Relationship breakdown.
    Trauma.
    Transitions from care or the justice system.

Homelessness is not personal failure. It is a systems failure. And it is preventable.
Our response

Kids Under Cover exists to prevent and end youth homelessness before it starts.

We do this by supporting young people to remain safely housed, stay connected to family, community and education, and in
turn build pathways to stability and independence.

Preventing youth homelessness through early intervention and stable housing keeps vulnerable young people connected to
education, work, and community, stopping crises before they begin. Programs like our Studio Program and Village 21 model
reduce long-term social and economic costs, while protecting young people from lifelong disadvantage and supporting them
to thrive.

What the data shows

The 2024–25 SHS data highlights four clear themes:
• Young people are entering homelessness alone and in crisis, often after violence or sudden eviction.
• Support is short-term, offering safety in the moment but rarely long-term stability.
• Education and opportunity are disrupted, entrenching disadvantage into adulthood.
• These are systemic gaps- not individual failings. A lack of early intervention, youth-appropriate housing, and holistic
  support drives repeat presentations.


Why this matters

• The trends are clear: the current system reacts to crisis rather than preventing it. Young people need stable housing, early
  support, and pathways that keep them connected to school, community, and opportunity.

• This context underscores the importance of Kids Under Cover’s preventative work- helping young people avoid the crisis
  point altogether and build a safer, more secure future.
OUR IMPACT IN FY25

Studio program

Kids Under Cover’s studio program continued to provide over 1000 young people at risk of homelessness with safe, stable
accommodation every night. The program furthered its expansion into South Australia during the year, building an additional
20 new studios, bringing the total number of studios constructed in South Australia to 40. This expansion was made possible
through funding from the South Australian Housing Trust, together with generous contributions from JB HiFi’s Workplace Giving
program and philanthropy such as Foundation Barossa.

Mobile Pods

The Mobile Pod pilot continued to expand over the year through the generous support of the Paul Ramsay Foundation. The
program provides a more rapid accommodation solution for young people at imminent risk of homelessness, with a focus on
those who have experienced out-of-home care or engagement with the youth justice system. A new type of backyard pod has
been designed to be constructed on site, in backyards where a pod on wheels could not access.

A real time evaluation of the pilot is currently underway and the impact of the pods is beginning to be seen, with early data
demonstrating the benefits for young people and families. Read more about this in section 9.

Scholarships

Kids Under Cover provided financial assistance to support 119 young people currently accessing our studio program to better
engage in learning and work towards their higher education and employment goals. Families report that KUC scholarships
increased their ability to purchase necessary educational resources, with almost half also reporting that the scholarship
increased the young person’s desire to engage with learning, as well as to be inspired to make plans for their future learning or
work pathways.

We also awarded a further 355 scholarships as part of the DFFH Student Scholarship Program, assisting students who are at
risk of homelessness or living in social housing to complete their Year 11 or 12 studies.

These scholarships were reported to relieve financial stress for almost all recipients, with over 70% also reporting that the
scholarships assisted in improving the student’s attendance and future career aspirations, and over 80% agreeing it
had a positive impact on academic performance, social inclusion and well-being of the young person.

Kids Under Cover is excited to be partnering with GEGAC (Gippsland and East Gippsland Aboriginal Cooperative) and

STORIES OF IMPACT

Mobile pod pilot case study

The provision of a mobile pod created an opportunity to safely trial family reunification for a
15-year-old male following time in out-of-home care. This reunification would otherwise not
have been possible due to concerns about his influence on the safety and well-being of his
siblings.

Over the course of its six month placement, KUC’s mobile pod provided a separate dedicated
space for this young person to return home, offering privacy and a secure environment where
he could better regulate his emotions. It also helped reduce tension and conflict within the
household, while supporting the young person’s progress toward stability, independence and
building his capacity to make better life choices.

Since living in the mobile pod, the carer has observed noticeable improvements in the young
person’s behaviour and emotional wellbeing, and was pleased to report that he was not engaged
in any offending. He is looking for work and is focused on improving his health and wellbeing.
He is enjoying an improved relationship with his mum and her partner, has had an opportunity to
undertake work experience and has more time to think about what he would like his future to be
like. He believes he is making more positive decisions for himself and managing how he lives. He
is spending more time doing things that interest him and is feeling like he has greater peace of
mind.

The young person’s caseworker shared with KUC that the mobile pod has helped the young
person break out of his cycle of criminal offending and encouraged him to start focusing
on his future. He is no longer associating with peers he was committing crimes with and is
building resilience to better cope with life challenges. He is also building independent living
skills and relationships with friends. The caseworker also reported that the mobile pod has
greatly supported her work by enabling higher quality tailored support to be provided to the
young person and the family, and freeing up her time and resources spent trying to source
an alternative accommodation solution. She felt better able to meet the range of needs of the
young person once they had safe and stable accommodation in place, and that she was able to
focus on and strengthen (service delivery) partnerships, to increase positive outcomes for the
young person and all the family.

All of these factors have contributed to diverting this young person from future incarceration
and formed the foundations to create a brighter future. The mobile pod was in place and used
by the young person for almost six months. Following its deployment and success in enabling
family reunification, Child Protection were able to source a long-term housing solution for the
young person. This mobile pod can now be re-deployed to help the next young person in need.
LOOKING AHEAD

As we move into FY26 we are focused on building momentum and preparing for the next phase of our work. A key priority for
the year ahead will be the development and adoption of a new Strategic Plan, which will guide our future direction through
four proposed pillars: Focused Sustainable Growth; Co-design and Youth Voice; Innovate for Impact; and Thought
Leadership and Advocacy.

This work will strengthen our commitment to growth, inclusion, innovation and influence in preventing youth homelessness.

We have many existing and new trusts and foundations supporting our work and innovation. We will also actively pursue
government investment to assist another 450 more vulnerable young Victorians in 300 new studios, secure resources to
support studio relocations, and expand our Village21 and Cluster Model projects through federal funding. These priorities will
enable Kids Under Cover to scale our impact and provide more young people with the safe, stable housing they need to thrive.
