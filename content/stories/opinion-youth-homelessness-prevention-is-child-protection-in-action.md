*By Stephen Nash, CEO, Kids Under Cover*

> **What if we measured child protection success not only by how we respond to crisis, but by how effectively we prevent it?**

This National Child Protection Week, the theme _Every Conversation Matters: Shifting Conversation to Action_ asks us to consider what meaningful action to protect children and young people can look like.

### At Kids Under Cover, we believe that conversation must include youth homelessness prevention.

For many young people, homelessness does not begin with a single event. Pressure builds over time. Overcrowding, housing instability and conflict within the home can place increasing strain on young people, their families and carers.

> If we wait until a young person leaves home, we’ve waited until those pressures have reached a critical point.

Earlier intervention can create the conditions for a young person to remain safely connected to family or carers, education and the support networks already around them.

Sometimes that intervention is very practical: creating a safe, stable space to foster connection and build independence. **At Kids Under Cover, this means practical housing solutions that respond to young people at different points of need.**

Our [Studio Program](https://www.kuc.org.au/our-programs/studio-program/) provides additional accommodation at the home of a young person’s family or carer, creating more space that can help relieve pressure within the home while keeping them connected to the people and support around them.

> The program currently houses over 300 young people each night who are in contact with Child Protection, and with government investment, we can scale even further.

For young people transitioning from care, our [Village 21](https://www.kuc.org.au/our-programs/village21/) model is specifically designed to support them away from being entrenched in the system and towards independence, rather than a trajectory towards homelessness.

The results reported by carers reinforce the value of this practical intervention.

In our 2025 Carer Survey, conducted by RFI Global:

> 68% of carers said the Studio had resolved all overcrowding issues in their household.

That makes a Kids Under Cover Studio more than just _bricks and mortar_.

It’s one example of what homelessness prevention can look like before a young person reaches crisis.

**This points to a broader question about where governments, funders and service systems invest their effort.**

> Responding effectively when a child is unsafe will always be essential, but a strong child protection system should also address the conditions that can push young people and families towards crisis.

Investing earlier can help preserve safe relationships and reduce the likelihood that problems escalate across homelessness, child protection, health and family violence services.

National Child Protection Week reminds us that every conversation matters. The next step is turning those conversations into practical action that strengthens families, supports carers and gives young people a safe place to stay connected.

Youth homelessness prevention doesn’t belong at the edge of the child protection conversation, it should be recognised as part of how we protect young people before homelessness begins.

**Preventing youth homelessness is child protection in action.**
