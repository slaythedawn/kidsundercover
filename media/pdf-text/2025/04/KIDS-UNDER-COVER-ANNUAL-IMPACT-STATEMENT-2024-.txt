MESSAGE FROM THE CEO/ CHAIR
Welcome to our 2024 Annual Impact Statement
We are excited to present to you our newly streamlined format for annual impact reporting. It’s been an incredible year for KUC ﬁlled with meaningful milestones and
progress:

• Village 21 – In partnership with VACCA, we successfully completed and launched Moorumbina Mongurnallin- a ﬂagship project. It's dedicated to helping Aboriginal
young people transition from care and the early results are already showing tremendous impact.

• Mobile Pod pilot: We’ve also launched a pilot for Mobile Pods, designed to provide families with rapid, emergency support to prevent breakdowns before they
escalate, and quick placement after time in out-of-home care or the justice system.

Our Studios continue to make a life changing impact, providing crucial supported accommodation to young people at risk of leaving home prematurely or needing a
placement after out-of-home care. We are deeply motivated by the difference our programs make in their lives and the lives of the families we serve.

While we are proud to house over 1,000 young people each night we can only meet 5% of the overwhelming demand. Sadly 95% of applicants- families in crisis and
young people at risk of homelessness – are left in crisis. It's painful to read the stories of those left behind and difficult to understand the lack of government support
for our proven Studio model, which has shown success in supporting young people avoid or achieve better placements from out-of-home care and youth justice.

Our model offers a proactive, preventative approach to homelessness, but the current focus on adult homelessness overlooks the root causes that leave vulnerable
young people at risk.

The good news is that homelessness can be prevented and even ended- but it requires a bold, comprehensive
strategy that emphasises prevention and early intervention. These are the missing ingredients in Victoria’s
approach, and this understanding is already driving change globally.

REFLECT RECONCILIATION ACTION PLAN
In 2024, Kids Under Cover proudly launched our ﬁrst Reﬂect Reconciliation Action Plan (RAP), marking a meaningful step forward in our
commitment to reconciliation. As the organisation expands its work alongside First Nations organisations and within Aboriginal and Torres
Strait Islander communities, it recognises the importance of aligning its values and practices with the core principles of reconciliation.

Central to this commitment is the belief that building trust with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander families and communities is essential
to supporting young people at risk of homelessness. Through the RAP, Kids Under Cover is embedding cultural learning, respect, and
inclusion throughout the organisation, ensuring its work is informed by the knowledge and experiences of First Nations peoples.

The organisation extends its gratitude to Reconciliation Australia for their guidance and support in the development of this plan and looks
forward to continuing this important journey in partnership with First Nations communities.

PROSPECTUS LAUNCH
In November, we launched our Prospectus, outlining investment
proposals to the Victorian government. These proposals highlight
opportunities to expand our impact, including the creation of
more studios and Village 21 projects — particularly for Aboriginal
young people and those at risk of entering out-of-home care or
the youth justice system. Achieving systemic change requires
strong partnerships, and we invite our supporters to join us in this
mission—through funding, advocacy, and collaboration.

The bonus with these investments is that they not only provide
brighter futures for vulnerable young people, they stem the ﬂow
of the largest cohort who end up in entrenched adult
homelessness.
   KEY FINDINGS FROM OUR ANNUAL CARER SURVEY
   Key Findings from Our Annual Carer Survey
   This year’s carer survey provided valuable insights into the needs and challenges faced by carers and families. These ﬁndings will
   inform our future strategies and focus areas as we work to better support our communities.

   Every young person deserves a safe place to grow, feel connected and build a future. Kids Under Cover’s innovative studio program is
   making a life-changing difference for over 1,000 young people at risk of homelessness every night in Victoria and South Australia. By
   providing secure, supported housing, we’re helping to break cycles of family conﬂict, improve educational outcomes and build
   independence.

   A recent study by RFI Global (January 2025), involving 166 families and carers, reveals just how transformative our studios have been.
   Young people are not only gaining a stable home—they're regaining control of their futures. From reduced time spent away from home
   to stronger community connections, improved mental well-being and better engagement in learning, the impact is profound.

   By listening to the voices of carers and young people, we see ﬁrst-hand the powerful role that early intervention plays in preventing
   homelessness before it starts. These results affirm that secure, stable housing is more than just a roof over their heads—it’s a
   foundation to build brighter futures.

SCHOLARSHIP PROGRAM OVERVIEW
Our scholarship program continues to break down barriers for young
people facing ﬁnancial challenges.

In 2024, we awarded 278 scholarships: 10 for primary school, 227
for secondary school, and 41 for higher education.

These scholarships help young people pursue their education, achieve
their career goals, and break the cycle of disadvantage.



SUPPORTER ENGAGEMENT
In November we had the privilege of hosting our Partnering for Impact
event, generously supported by Corrs Chambers Westgarth. At the
event, we discussed the growing issue of youth homelessness in Victoria,
where over 7,600 young people are affected each night, and the urgent
need for funding in prevention and early intervention.

We were honoured to hear from The Hon. Harriet Shing MP, who spoke
about the importance of systemic change in addressing homelessness.
Corey Harrison from VACCA (Victorian Aboriginal Child and Community
Agency) also discussed the value of our collaboration to provide
culturally supportive spaces for young people.

A powerful moment of the evening came when Aristea, a young woman
supported by our studio and scholarship programs, shared how our
support has transformed her life and future including the imminent
completion of her law degree.
   LOOKING AHEAD
   There is a constant stream of vulnerable young people ﬂowing towards and becoming stuck in homelessness and on a trajectory to remain
   stuck for decades. Victoria continues to pour vast taxpayer funds into responding to the increasing demand for adults in homelessness
   crisis. Without investment in prevention and early intervention, simply pouring more funds downstream to respond to the adult
   homelessness crisis will never reduce homelessness beyond the lucky few who manage to secure housing. Kids Under Cover is pleased to
   be working with new partners and coalitions to pilot solutions for care leavers and young people at risk of or exiting youth justice.
