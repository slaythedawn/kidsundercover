ABOUT US

SS

Leading the way in early intervention and
prevention, Kids Under Cover supports young
people between the ages of 12 and 25 years,
who are experiencing or at risk of homelessness.
Since 1989 we have been delivering programs
that are life-changing for entire families.

The Studio Program is at the heart of Kids Under Cover's
work. One or two-bedroom studios (with a bathroom) are

installed in the backyard of a family or carer's home, giving
young people their own secure and stable space to thrive.

These young people (and those eligible living in the main
home) can then access a Kids Under Cover Scholarship to
assist with the financial costs associated with education or
training and to help them realise their goals.

Kids Under Cover has accomplished a lot since 1989 — but
whilst there are young people who are experiencing or
at risk of homelessness, there is still much to be done.

STUDIO GROWTH

This year we built 101 studios, providing safe and

secure accommodation for 140 at-risk young people. By
working closely with our Community Service Organisation
partners, we have been able to have a transformative
impact in the lives of these young people and their
households. Our studios do more than just provide space
~ they give young people a sense of independence and
support those engaged in education with a place to study.

This brings the total number of studios on the ground
at the end of this financial year to 552. During the year
we retired 20 studios and houses, due to their age and
condition. Refer to page 10 for an overview of our studios.

Our Studio Program is made possible thanks to the
generous support of our Community, Corporate and
Philanthropic Partners, along with the support of the
Victorian Government. Their shared vision of a future
where no young person experiences homelessness
enables us to continue providing stable and secure
accommodation that changes lives.

PROUDLY
AUSTRALIAN MADE

We are proud that our studios are 100% Australian
made with 100% Australian materials. Our studios are
made with Durra Panel, an environmentally sustainable
material. Durra Panel is an innovative building material
made from the agricultural waste by-product from
farming that would otherwise be burnt contributing to
carbon emissions. Our studios are manufactured locally
by Ortech, a family business based in Bendigo that we
have been working with for over 25 years.

IT TAKES A VILLAGE

Village 21 is designed to build life skills and
provide opportunities for young people exiting
out-of-home care when they reach the age of 18.

A first-of-its-kind, Village 21 is a scaled-up, co-located
version of the existing Kids Under Cover Studio Program.
By providing accommodation combined with wrap around
supports, Village 21 will assist at-risk young people aged
18-21 years who are leaving out-of-home care or are
already post care.

The project will provide studios for six young people to
have stable accommodation, with support and communal
living elements for up to three years. The Department
of Health and Human Services has funded Village 21
through Accommodation for the Homeless Phase 2,
which is part of the Victorian Government's $109 million
homelessness support investment. The social outcomes
sought for young people from this project include no
longer being at risk of or experiencing homelessness,
remaining in safe and supported accommodation,
learning independent living skills and building and
maintaining healthy and functional relationships.

This year we identified land in Preston which was an ideal
location for the project, with close proximity to public
transport, shopping and other community amenities, as
well as being located in a suitable residential area. The
City of Darebin undertook a public consultation process

to change the use of the land for our project. The public’s
response to this consultation and the Village 21 project
was overwhelmingly positive. City of Darebin granted us a
three-year peppercorn lease and we have commenced the
planning permit process for the site with the local council.

The design for Village 21 has been undertaken by NH
Architecture, and we thank them for their work and
support. NH Architecture have redesigned our current
studio layout to meet the needs of this specific project.

Artist’s impressions of Village 21.

They have designed a communal living concept that will
support the development of a thriving community for the
young people involved in this project.

We are working towards launching Village 21 in late
2019-20 and are excited for the significantly improved
social outcomes for the young people who will be part
of the project

Colin Radford, Kids Under Cover Chair, shared details

COMMUNITY PARTNERS

Community Partnerships are imperative to the work

Gabriele, her husband and their four daughters were
living under significant financial stress in a small three-
bedroom Office of Housing property in Seaford. The
parents initially occupied the main bedroom, their older
daughters (aged 21 and 16) shared the second bedroom,
and the younger daughters (aged 11 and 9) shared the
third bedroom

However, Gabriele’s health conditions made it impossible
to continue to share a bedroom, and so her husband slept

--- page break ---
in the lounge room, adding to the overcrowding in the
family home. The home's only shared living space had to

« Life was very cluttered, With the four girls
and us parents, there was so much stress inside the
house. There was just nowhere at all fo escape each
other” says Gabriele, 5)

Briana, 16 years old, found the tension and chaos too

The future is now looking bright for this family.

THE BOARD

The Kids Under Cover Board is responsible for the overall governance of the organisation
including its strategic direction, establishing goals for management and monitoring the
achievement of those goals. The board convenes a minimum of six times per year plus
an additional meeting at the Annual General Meeting (AGM)

Code of Conduct
Under the leadership of the Chair and the CEO, and within the boundaries of the
constitution, the board determines and articulates the values, vision and the strategic

direction of Kids Under Cover. In doing this, the board is expected to:

+ meet fiduciary responsibilities
as required under all relevant
commonwealth and state legislation;

* act within their duty of care to make
decisions in the best interests of
Kids Under Cover;

+ avoid conflicts of interest;

+ develop strategic planning and

direction of Kids Under Cover including
monitoring organisational performance

and evaluating strategic results;

= endorse internal policies;

* report back to the stakeholders
at the AGM;

* monitor the organisational compliance
with the relevant commonwealth and
state legislation and with Kids Under
Cover's own policies; and

+ evaluate its effectiveness as a board

(A) BASIS OF PREPARATION OF THE FINANCIAL REPORT
Historical Cost Convention

The financial report has been prepared on an accruals basis and is based on historical costs,

G) IMPAIRMENT OF NON-FINANCIAL ASSETS

Assets other than goodwill, intangible assets not yet ready for use and intangible
assets with indefinite useful lives are assessed for impairment whenever events or
circumstances arise that indicate the asset may be impaired.

An impairment loss is recognised when the carrying amount of an asset exceeds the
asset's recoverable amount. The recoverable amount of an asset is defined as the higher
of its fair value less costs to sell and value in use (where ‘value in use’ is determined as
the current replacement cost).

Impairment losses in respect of individual assets are recognised immediately in profit or
loss unless the asset is measured at a revalued amount, in which case the impairment

loss is treated as a revaluation decrease and is recognised in other comprehensive
income to the extent that it does not exceed the amount in the revaluation surplus
for the same class of asset.

A reversal of an impairment loss for an asset measured at cost is recognised in profit
or loss. A reversal of an impairment loss for an asset measured at a revalued amount
is treated as a revaluation increase and is recognised in other comprehensive income,
except to the extent that an impairment loss on the same class of asset was previously
recognised in profit or loss, in which case a reversal of that impairment loss is also
recognised in profit or loss.

NOTE 18: CHANGES IN ACCOUNTING POLICIES
(A) FINANCIAL INSTRUMENTS - ADOPTION OF AASB 9

AASB ? replaces AASB 139: Financial Instruments: Recognition and Measurement.
The key changes introduced by AASB 9 in relation to the accounting treatment
for financial instruments include:

+ simplifying the general classifications of financial assets into those measured
at amortised cost and those measured at fair value;

* permitting entities to irrevocably elect, on initial recognition, for gains and losses
on equity instruments not held for trading to be presented in other comprehensive
income (OCI);

+ simplifying the requirements for embedded derivatives, including removing the
requirement to separate and measure embedded derivatives at fair value, in relation
to embedded derivatives associated with financial assets measured at amortised cost;

+ requiring entities that elect to measure financial liabilities at fair value, to present
the portion of the change in fair value arising from changes in the entity's own
credit risk in OCI, except when it would create an ‘accounting mismatch’;

+ introducing a new model for hedge accounting that permits greater flexibility
in the ability to hedge risk, particularly with respect to non-financial items; and

+ introducing a new ‘expected credit loss’ impairment model (replacing the ‘incurred
loss’ impairment model of previous accounting standard)

Previously, unrealised gains and losses on managed funds were presented in OCI, these are
now recognised in the profit and loss. Other than this change, the application of AASB 9 has
not materially impacted the classification and measurement of the group's financial assets
and liabilities.

(B) REVENUE FROM CONTRACTS WITH CUSTOMERS - ADOPTION OF AASB 15
The group adopted AASB 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers from 1 July 2018.

AASB 15 provides (other than in relation to some specific exceptions, such as lease
contracts and insurance contracts) a single source of accounting requirements for all
contracts with customers, thereby replacing all current accounting pronouncements
on revenue. The core principle of AASB 15 is that an entity recognises revenue to depict
the transfer of promised goods or services to a customer in an amount that reflects the
consideration to which the entity expects to be entitled in exchange for those goods or
services. Under the previous accounting standard, AASB 118 Revenue, revenue from
the sale of goods was recognised when the significant risks and rewards of ownership
of the goods transferred to the buyer, and revenue from the rendering of services was
recognised by reference to the stage of completion of the transaction at the end of the
reporting period.

The Group's recognition and measurement of revenue from contracts with customers
has not changed as a result of the introduction of the new standard. Further details of
the group's accounting policies in relation to accounting for revenue from contracts with
customers under AASB 15 are contained in note 1.
