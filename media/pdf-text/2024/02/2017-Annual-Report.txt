OUR WORK

YOUTH
HOMELESSNESS

Nene

Young people usually don't fit the stereotype of
homelessness. They are rarely seen sleeping on
city streets, camped out in tents in a public park,
or in refuges and shelters.

Many young people couch-surf with friends or extended
family, or cram into overcrowded accommodation. They
may not be sleeping rough, but they're no less homeless.

Many believe they are better off living away from
home permanently. Sometimes the pressures of an
overcrowded home coupled with the burden of mental
health issues, trauma or grief, mean young people feel
like they don’t have a choice but to leave.

Australian Institute of Health and Welfare data shows
38,402 Victorians under the age of 25 sought assistance
from homelessness services last financial year, over 600
more than the previous year. Nearly 40% of Victorians
who sought homelessness help were under the age of 25.

Over 6,000 young Victorians who sought homelessness
assistance last year were couch-surfing.”

Housing affordability also plays a significant role
in homelessness.

Of the more than 67,700 properties listed for rent in
metropolitan and regional parts of Australia (on the
weekend of 1-2 of April 2017), just 29 were affordable
for a young person on Youth Allowance or Newstart?. If
young people feel they can no longer live at home, find
themselves couch-surfing regularly and are unable to
afford to rent a place to live, where are they to go?

OUR APPROACH TO TACKLING

YOUTH HOMELESSNESS

At Kids Under Cover we believe making a difference
starts with prevention.

We support young people most at risk of becoming homeless
due to overcrowding, family conflict, mental illness, housing
affordability, or a combination of these factors.

Our aim is to help relieve pressure points for young
people, keep them connected to family and the community,
encourage ongoing education or training, and maintain
social networks and ties to home.

Kids Under Cover's pre-emptive work of providing stable,
responsive accommodation for at-risk young people
has delivered proven prevention methods for almost
30 years. Early intervention has been successfully
shown to interrupt the cycle of homelessness, and

is a far more effective strategy both economically

and socially.

In partnership with more than 50 Community Service
Organisations (CSOs), we deliver an early intervention
strategy via our Studio Program

Complementing our Studio Program, the Kids Under
Cover Scholarship Program ensures young people are
supported to continue with education or training.

Since 1989 it's been Kids Under Cover's mission to
provide young people at risk of homelessness the

foundations to strengthen their connection to family,
community, and education.

STUDIO PROGRAM

The Studio Program is at the heart of Kids Under
Cover’s work. Relocatable one- or two-bedroom
(with bathroom) studios are built in the backyard
of a family or carer’s home. The studio provides
secure and stable accommodation for young
people at risk of homelessness.

Our studios support not only young people at risk of
homelessness but families at risk of breakdown, kinship
care and foster carers seeking additional space to
accommodate young people

The studio facilitates semi-independent living space for
the young person to study and a space to call their own.
Once the studio is no longer required, it can be relocated
to accommodate another young person in need.

The unique relocatable 6-Star energy efficiency rated
studios are built with Durra Panel, an environmentally
friendly product made from straw without additional
chemical binders. Studios can be constructed and
fully fitted within 10 days. This is thanks to the Ortech
designed flat-packed, pre-assembled frames and a
dedicated build team from Harris HMC.

Each studio has a life span of up to 20 years, they are
demountable and able to be relocated up to four times.

To ensure studios stay in top condition we monitor our

studios in a formal audit process. The audit data collected
also allows us to improve our budgeting and scheduling
of studio maintenance, ensuring maximum longevity of

the asset

With over 480 studios currently on the ground
(predominantly in Victoria) we have been able to
support more than 3,000 vulnerable and disadvantaged
young people between the ages of 12 and 25 years.

SOCIAL RETURN ON
INVESTMENT

EXECUTIVE
SUMMARY

Nee

Supported by EY (formally Ernst & Young), a Social Return
on Investment (SROI) analysis of the Kids Under Cover
programs was undertaken to determine the socio-economic
returns for all stakeholders affected by the issues of youth
homelessness and overcrowding. Returns over a six-year
period from 2010-2016 were considered.

The SRO! methodology was used to identify, measure and
value the impacts of the Kids Under Cover intervention for
each stakeholder, including young people and their carers
along with government and community sector partners

SROI is an internationally recognised, principles-based
approach for understanding and measuring the impacts
of a program or organisation. It provides a framework for
measuring and accounting for the broader concept of value.

KEY FINDINGS

The findings from the analysis are clear: the Kids
Under Cover Studio Program is effective in achieving
its aim of creating life-changing value for young people
living in overcrowded conditions and who are vulnerable
to homelessness

+ Most of the value of Kids Under Cover’s interventions
is felt directly by those assisted by the Studio or
Scholarship Programs, with EY calculating the social
return for those young people, which equates to over
$30 million over the last six years.

+ Kids Under Cover's benefits are felt more widely than just
by those young people identified as being at-risk. Other
young people over 12 years old within the household
have returned a social value greater than $16 million.

+ The social value returned to primary carers
(parent or carer of a young person) was greater
than $9 million.

The evidence provided within the SRO! analysis has shown:

+ For every dollar invested in Kids Under Cover over
the past six years, $4.17 of social value is returned.

+ Over six years the Kids Under Cover model generated
$63.129 million of social value.

+ The social value returned to the Government and to
Community Service Organisations (CSOs) was more
than $6 million across six years

+ The criminal justice system, health system and

specialist homeless services have benefited through

Kids Under Cover’s early intervention, resulting in

sizeable savings. The homeless service system,

providing emergency or short-term accommodation,
accrues the majority of these savings, relieving
pressure that would otherwise have cost $4 million
over the last six years.

The findings also show a Kids Under Cover intervention
can transform the lives of young people and their carers.

Lives of young people have improved as a result of the
Kids Under Cover Studio and Scholarship Programs.
The greatest improvements in the lives of young people
were in the following ways:

+ Decreased likelihood of homelessness
+ Improved interpersonal relationships

+ Improved emotional wellbeing and peace of mind
(reduced stress, greater safety and identity)

+ Improved independence

For the carers of young people, the results have also been
excellent. The positive outcomes for carers included:

+ Improved interpersonal relationships

+ Improved emotional wellbeing and peace of mind
(reduced stress, more personal time)

+ Improved social inclusion
+ Improved financial health

For Government, the results further evidence
prevention is better than cure. The state makes
sizeable savings in relation to:

+ Reduced costs in housing services (short term and
crisis accommodation)

+ Reduced costs to the health system

+ Reduced costs to the justice system

CSOs that provide support to families also benefit

from the Kids Under Cover interventions. The most

material outcome for CSOs is improved efficiency

(better case-management and capacity) in delivering

their services. CSOs spend less time, on average, with

each family and have more time and ability to support

other families.

SCHOLARSHIP PROGRAM

Education is one of the most important investments
a country can make in the life of its young people;
it is critical to reducing poverty and inequality.
At an individual level, it has a significant positive
impact on a young person's confidence, self-
esteem and employment prospects.

Kids Under Cover's strategy of complementing the
provision of studio accommodation with educational
support has proven to be a simple yet effective approach
to the long-term prevention of youth homelessness.

The Scholarship Program helps young people who have
the passion and motivation to continue education, but

don't have the resources. These scholarships are helping
young people aged 15 to 25 years old continue with their
schooling, TAFE, university and training.

ABOUT OUR SCHOLARSHIP PROGRAM
The Kids Under Cover Scholarship Program provides
financial assistance for items such as school books,
stationery, computers, uniforms or fees.

Our scholarships give young people the support they need
to stay in school. This is not just by helping financially
with educational expenses, but also by preventing the
social exclusion that can haunt young people who miss
out on camps, excursions or other school social activities
due to lack of funds.

Ascholarship can also provide a young person with
the freedom to choose the subjects they are passionate
about, rather than just those they can afford, thus keeping
them engaged with their studies.

The Kids Under Cover Scholarship Program is available
to young people residing in our studios when enrolled

in school, TAFE, university or training. Their siblings and
any other young people living in the main home are also
eligible to apply.

STUDIO EVALUATION 2016/17

To properly understand the impact of our early
intervention and prevention model each year we
undertake an evaluation of the Studio Program.

For the past three years we have worked closely with
research company, DBM Consultants, to interview carers
of young people who were residing in a studio.

Eight interviewers spoke with 120 carers, conducting a
10-minute survey across a five-day period. This number
represents 25% of our client base, giving us a solid
understanding of the impact our programs are having
on the young people we support.

These interviews have also provided us with a greater
understanding of the benefits delivered for carers of young
people and the positive changes experienced in family life
since the installation of the studio.

The research showed a strong impact on the well-being of
the young person and family after the studio was installed,
particularly regarding reduced crowding in the house,
increased ability to get along and improvements in the
young person's achievements at school.

JOE WHITE BEQUEST

Joe White was born in 1873 in Ballarat, by the time he was
19, he had joined his father’s business, Crane and White
Maltsters, to learn the art of making malt. At 23, upon
his father's death, Joe took over and renamed the then-
struggling business, and under his leadership it prospered.

In conjunction with his burgeoning business, Joe was
active in local community activities and charitable
endeavours, and subsequently moved from Ballarat
to Melbourne, settling in Brighton.

In 1951, the company was floated as a public company under

the name of Joe White Maltings Limited with Joe as Chairman
and Managing Director. When he died in 1959 aged 86, the

Joe White Bequest was born to ensure the continuation of his
strong commitment to community and charity.

Nearly 60 years on, the Joe White Bequest has distributed
over $30 million to Victorian hospitals, benevolent
institutions and charities, establishing itself as one of
the state's most long-standing philanthropic entities
and changing the lives of countless Victorians

Kids Under Cover have been a grateful beneficiary of the
Joe White Bequest's support since 2008. During that time,
it has fully or co-funded the build of 12 Kids Under Cover

--- page break ---
studios, providing vital accommodation for over 20 young
people formerly at risk of homelessness.

In 2016 the Bequest's grant fully funded the construction of a
new one-bedroom studio in Hastings, and contributed to the
build of another two studios, one in Hillside and another in
Brown Hill.

The Brown Hill studio provided accommodation for a single
mother and her three children escaping domestic violence.
After time in refuges, the fractured family was finally
able to secure public housing. Though the home was too
small, it at least provided a chance to sink some roots in a
new community; but recovery from the past trauma in an
overcrowded space was proving too much for the oldest of
the three children. Only the provision of a Kids Under Cover
studio ensured the family could remain together to heal

The support of the Joe White Bequest for Kids Under Cover
has enabled this family, and many others, to start anew, and
in the process, offer young people recovering from trauma
and experiencing disadvantage to maximise their potential.
And for this, Kids Under Cover is forever grateful.

WOOD FAMILY FOUNDATION

The Wood Family Foundation was established in 2005 by the
generosity of Mr James and Mrs Margaret Wood with the
specific purpose of “providing support where needed”.

Arriving in Australia from England, via New Zealand, they
worked hard which bought some success. James and
Margaret believed they had been fortunate in life and wanted
to give back to the community and accordingly set up the
Wood Family Foundation. It is run solely by family members.
It was James and Margaret's wish that the foundation live
on in perpetuity, providing support for Australians in crisis,
ensuring environmental sustainability, funding much needed
medical research and assisting third world communities.

The Wood Family Foundation is proud to be a long-term
contributor to Kids Under Cover, supporting young people
who are potentially at risk of a lifetime of poverty.

With support stretching back over 10 years, the foundation
has provided grants to Kids Under Cover on an almost
annual basis, co-funding numerous studios across Victoria,
including in Morwell, Bundoora, Cockatoo, Wendouree,
Wangaratta and Hastings

The foundation's impact has been significant. As a direct
result of their generosity, six young people have been
diverted from the risk of homelessness. Indirectly, each

space provided, minimising the chance of those families
breaking down

The studio enabled by the Wood Family Foundation on the
Mornington Peninsula in 2016 meant a 19-year-old young
man with a troubled and complex upbringing could access
secure and stable accommodation for the first time in
many years.

Overcrowding in the family home, coupled with mental
health issues, and a fractured family life impacted by
domestic violence, placed him at significant risk of
homelessness. A one-bedroom studio co-funded by the
Wood Family Foundation and three other philanthropic
supporters provided this young man with a sanctuary near
his mother and younger siblings, helping to eliminate the
risk of future family breakdown.

Kids Under Cover is humbled by the long-term confidence
the Wood Family Foundation has placed in our vision of a

community where no young person is homeless, and hopes
to continue working together towards this goal in the future.

THE FLEW FOUNDATION

Contrary to some perceptions, philanthropy is not about
donating money. It's about building relationships and trust,
with view to achieving a shared social purpose.

It is no accident the word ‘trust’ permeates the philanthropic
landscape. A philanthropic gift is ultimately a leap of faith,
and if a charity can honour this commitment, then together
the two parties can make a world of difference. Kids Under
Cover's emerging relationship with the Flew Foundation is
a salient example of this.

The Flew Foundation is a small family entity established
in 2003. The foundation's purpose is to assist in areas of
community need where they can make a difference, being
conscious of rural as well as urban issues, with a preference
to work with visionary and professional organisations that
focus on youth and target specific medical research needs.
The foundation gives emphasis to issues where opportunity
is compromised by circumstance.

The foundation first resolved to support Kids Under Cover
in 2012, and as one director said: “We have been part of this
journey with Kids Under Cover for six years now because
it continues to address the needs of disadvantaged youth
in a very specific way. The people involved are committed,
enthusiastic and professional, and above all get results.”

To build the understanding between funder and charity, the
directors accepted an invitation to visit a Kids Under Cover
studio in 2013. Here they met the young people residing
in the studio and their grandmother, seeing first-hand the
impact a studio can have on a young person's life, and by
extension, the whole family. The reassurance and validation
gained from the studio visit, in the words of the directors,
“won us over”, and saw subsequent donations provided in
2013, 2014 and 2015.

Afterward they attended the Cubby House Challenge”
auction event in 2016, and not only witnessed the excitement
of the auction, but had the opportunity to meet with other
supporters of Kids Under Cover.

The support from the Flew Foundation extended into both
2016 and 2017, with the most recent donation co-funding the
build of a one-bedroom studio in Bairnsdale. This 2017 Flew

Foundation donation is being coupled with funds from three

other philanthropic supporters to enable the studio build.
In doing so, it embodies Kids Under Cover's desire for true
partnerships with its philanthropic supporters, and a shared
vision of a community where no young person is homeless.

THE BOARD

The Kids Under Cover Board is responsible for the overall governance of the organisation
including its strategic direction, establishing goals for management and monitoring the
achievement of those goals. The board convenes a minimum of six times per year, plus
an additional meeting at the Annual General Meeting (AGM).

Code of Conduct

Under the leadership of the Chair and the CEO, and within the boundaries of the
constitution, the board determines and articulates the values, vision and the strategic
direction of Kids Under Cover. In doing this, the board is expected to:

+ meet fiduciary responsibilities
as required under all relevant
commonwealth and state legislation;

* act within their duty of care to make
decisions in the best interests of Kids
Under Cover;

+ avoid conflicts of interest;

+ develop strategic planning and
direction of Kids Under Cover including
monitoring organisational performance
and evaluating strategic results;

+ endorse internal policies;

* report back to the stakeholders
at the AGM;

* monitor the organisational compliance
with the relevant commonwealth and
state legislation and with Kids Under
Cover's own policies; and

+ evaluate its effectiveness as a board.

NOTE 1: STATEMENT OF SIGNIFICANT ACCOUNTING POLICIES

The financial report is a general purpose financial report that has been prepared in
accordance with the Australian Charities and Not for profits Commission Act 2012
and Australian Accounting Standards Reduced Disclosure Requirements, Interpretations
and other applicable authoritative pronouncements of the Australian Accounting
Standards Board.

The financial report covers Kids Under Cover as an individual entity. Kids Under Cover is
a company limited by shares, incorporated and domiciled in Australia. Kids Under Cover
is a not for profit entity for the purpose of preparing the financial statements.

The financial report was approved by the directors as at the date of the directors’ report.

The following are the significant accounting policies adopted by the company in the
preparation and presentation of the financial report. The accounting policies have been
consistently applied, unless otherwise stated.

(A) BASIS OF PREPARATION OF THE FINANCIAL REPORT
Historical Cost Convention

The financial report has been prepared on an accruals basis and is based on historical
costs, modified, where applicable, by the measurement at fair value of selected non
current assets, financial assets and financial liabilities. Cost is based on the fair values
of the consideration given in exchange for assets.

(B) INCOME TAX

No provision for income tax has been raised as the company is exempt from income tax
under Division 50 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997.

(C) REVENUE

Investment income

Investment income comprises of interest revenue and is recognised when it accrues.
In kind donations

Goods and services donated to the company are included at fair value to the company
where this can be quantified. No amounts are included in the financial statements

for services donated by volunteers. Goods donated for resale, raffle and auction are
included as income when they are sold.

Asset sales

The gain or loss on disposal of all non current assets is determined as the difference
between the carrying amount of the asset at the time of disposal and the net proceeds
on disposals.

All revenue is measured net of the amount of goods and services tax (GST).

(D) CONTRIBUTIONS GOVERNMENT GRANTS AND DONATIONS

Anon reciprocal contribution or grant is recognised when the entity obtains control of
the contribution or grant and it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the
entity, and the amount of the contribution or grant can be measured reliably.

If conditions attached to the contribution or grant that must be satisfied before the entity
is eligible to receive the contribution, recognition of contribution or income is deferred
until those conditions are met.

Anon reciprocal donation is recognised when the right to receive a donation has
been established.

(G) IMPAIRMENT

At the end of each reporting period, the company assesses whether there is any
indication that an asset may be impaired. The assessment will include considering
external sources of information and internal sources of information. If such an indication
exists, an impairment test is carried out on the asset by comparing the recoverable
amount of the asset, being the higher of the asset's fair value less costs to sell and
value in use, to the asset's carrying value. Any excess of the asset's carrying value
over its recoverable amount is expensed to the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

Depreciated replacement costs is used to determine value in use. Depreciated
replacement costs is the current replacement cost of an asset less, where applicable,
accumulated depreciation to date, calculated on the basis of such cost.

(H) EMPLOYEE BENEFITS
(i) Short term employee benefit obligations

Liabilities arising in respect of wages and salaries, annual leave and any other employee
benefits (other than termination benefits) expected to be settled wholly before twelve
months after the end of the annual reporting period are measured at the (undiscounted)
amounts based on remuneration rates which are expected to be paid when the liability is
settled. The expected cost of short term employee benefits in the form of compensated
absences such as annual leave is recognised in the provision for employee benefits

All other short term employee benefit obligations are presented as payables in the
statement of financial position.

(ii) Long term employee benefit obligations

The provision for other long term employee benefits, including obligations for long
service leave and annual leave, which are not expected to be settled wholly before
twelve months after the end of the reporting period, are measured at the present value
of the estimated future cash outflow to be made in respect of the services provided by
employees up to the reporting date. Expected future payments incorporate anticipated

future wage and salary levels, durations of service and employee turnover, and are
discounted at rates determined by reference to market yields at the end of the reporting
period on high quality corporate bonds that are denominated in the currency in which
the benefits will be paid, and that have terms approximating to the terms of the
related obligation. For currencies in which there is no deep market in such high quality
corporate bonds, the market yields (at the end of the reporting period) on government
bonds denominated in that currency are used. Any remeasurements for changes in
assumptions of obligations for other long term employee benefits are recognised in
profit or loss in the periods in which the change occurs.

Other long term employee benefit obligations are presented as current liabilities in the
statement of financial position if the entity does not have an unconditional right to defer
settlement for at least twelve months after the reporting date, regardless of when the
actual settlement is expected to occur. All other long term employee benefit obligations
are presented as non current liabilities in the statement of financial position.

(I) LEASES

Leases are classified at their inception as either operating or finance leases based
on the economic substance of the agreement so as to reflect the risks and benefits
incidental to ownership.

(J) GOODS AND SERVICES TAX (GST)

Revenues, expenses and purchased assets are recognised net of the amount of GST,
except where the amount of GST incurred is not recoverable from the Tax Office. In these
circumstances the GST is recognised as part of the cost of acquisition of the asset or as
part of an item of the expense. Receivables and payables in the statement of financial
position are shown inclusive of GST.

Cash flows are presented in the statement of cash flows on a gross basis, except for the
GST component of investing and financing activities, which are disclosed as operating
cash flows

consistency with current year disclosures.

The company is dependent upon the ongoing receipt of grants and donations to ensure
the continuance of its studio and scholarship programs.
